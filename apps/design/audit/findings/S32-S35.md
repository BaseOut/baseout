## 4 Primary action

**Settings has no primary action, by design, and that is right.** Every row commits itself
(`settingsControls.ts:141-164`); a hub-level Save would be the lie `pattern-settings-row` usageDont
already forbids. **ACCEPT.**

What is wrong is the *weighting* of the actions that do exist: `SettingsView.astro:192` gives
`button`/`avatar` rows `btn-soft btn-primary` and `:201-203` gives `link` rows `btn-ghost`. On
Account that makes **Change photo** (opens a picker) visually equal to **Sign out everywhere** (ends
every session) and heavier than **View sessions**. The ladder encodes the control's *kind*, not its
*consequence* → **F13**.

Billing's primary action is **Change plan** — a `btn-ghost` link row on a category whose own scope
line reads `Per org · admin`, pointing at a screen that does not exist. Help has no action at all.

## 7 Cross-surface consistency

Measured against the reference surfaces **Schema · Data · Reports** and, for the editing contract,
against the audit's named reference `SourceDetailView`.

- **Save contract.** `audit/decisions/30-one-save-contract.md`: Read → explicit Edit → Save+Cancel in
  one slot → Cancel reverts in place. `SourceDetailView.astro:16-22` implements it with the canonical
  `.sch-modeswitch`. **Settings uses none of it** — it uses commit-on-blur/change with a `Saved`
  flash (`settingsControls.ts:4-17`). This is defensible and I do not file it as a defect: the
  catalog blesses it (`pattern-settings-row` §"The commit contract"), independent knobs genuinely
  differ from a document, and `Escape` reverts. **The one place it breaks down is F4** — a contract
  with no Edit switch has to make "this cannot be edited" visible some other way, and it does not.
- **`grep -rn beforeunload apps/web/src` → 0 hits, app-wide.** Confirmed. No exit is gated anywhere,
  including Settings. On Settings this is **harmless and correct**: a change is committed the moment
  focus leaves the field, so there is no dirty state to lose. Recorded so the lead can see the grep
  was run and its result reasoned about, not just reported. D30's `beforeunload` clause belongs to
  `SchemaDocs`, not here.
- **Destructive confirm.** Settings uses `ConfirmModal` + `CONFIRM_DESTRUCTIVE` + the `.reg-danger`
  border — **identical** to `SourceDetailView.astro:202,271` and `DestinationDetailView`. This is the
  best cross-surface agreement on the page. Do not touch.
- **Page header.** `pattern-page-header` (`storybook.ts:2971`, authored 2026-08-13) states every page
  opens with `<h1 class="text-title">` and that this class is what the 1280 boundary hides so the app
  bar can paint the page name. `/schema`, `/reports`, `/data` all render one (measured). `/settings`,
  `/settings/billing`, `/help` render **zero** → **F7**.
- **Empty state.** `pattern-empty-state` governs; Settings under `trial`/`empty` renders no empty
  state at all, it renders confident fabricated content instead → **F1**. Compare `SourcesView` under
  `?fixture=empty`, which does the right thing.
- **Tier gating.** `decision-no-tier-gating-default` + **D14**. Ruling below.
- **Badges.** `badge-ghost` at `SettingsView.astro:222` — ghost is not one of the semantic variants
  the standing rule names. Instance of the app-wide badge rule, listed in §5, not filed separately.

## THE BILLING RULING, against `decision-no-tier-gating-default`

**The question:** does Billing gate, upsell, or merely inform?

**The answer: it neither gates nor upsells — and it does not inform either.** All three, and the
third is the finding.

- **It does not gate.** No row in the Billing category is disabled, locked or hidden by plan. The one
  live control (`Allow overage`, `settingsCatalog.ts:400-408`) commits for anyone. Consistent with
  `decision-no-tier-gating-default`.
- **It does not upsell.** No "Upgrade to Pro" anywhere in `settingsCatalog.ts`. The one plan-changing
  row is a neutral `Change plan` link. Consistent with the decision.
- **It does not inform.** `Current plan` and `Usage this month` are **doors with no values behind
  them** — computed pane text contains no plan name under any fixture. A billing surface that cannot
  answer "which plan am I on" has not informed; it has deferred → **F3**.

**Against D28.** D28 found four AI consumers collapsing `ready | locked | no-credits` into two, so a
paying Pro customer out of credits is told to buy Pro. **Billing does not have that shape** — it has
no union to collapse, because it renders no plan state at all. That is a *different* and, for this
surface, worse failure: D28's surfaces are wrong about the state, Billing is silent about it. The
same root cause is visible though — **there is no plan/capability value plumbed into `apps/web` for
any surface to read**, which is why D28's consumers had to guess and why Billing shows nothing.
Whether a plan value exists in the production backend is **NOT-OURS**; that Billing renders no slot
for one is ours.

**Against D14.** D14 §3 says "no gate lands on a placeholder". Every one of the app's upgrade CTAs
still lands on `/settings/billing`, which is still the 13-line placeholder — measured today
(`SchemaHealth.astro:199,503,580` · `SchemaChat.astro:185` · `CadencePicker.astro:40` ·
`BaseSelectionTable.astro:385,608`). **D14 is unshipped on its destination half.** The new fact D14
did not have: **a real Billing surface already exists at `/settings?tab=billing`**, so "build or
demote" now has a third and cheapest option — *retarget*. And `settingsCatalog.ts:481` is a ninth
gate dialect D14's census missed → **F10**.

**Ruling:** Billing is **compliant with `decision-no-tier-gating-default` in what it does and
non-compliant in what it omits.** The decision says locks are capability, not price — Billing shows
neither capability nor price. Nothing here needs to be removed to satisfy the decision; a plan value
and a usage value need to be added, and the eleven CTAs need to stop pointing at an empty page.

## What is good here — do not touch

1. **The `?tab=` state contract, and especially `buildSettingsLinks`** (`settingsCatalog.ts:519-567`).
   One parameter carries the category, the drill state and the back link; **every unrelated query
   parameter is carried forward**, so `?fixture=empty` survives a category change. No second store,
   no history shim, no script. This is the cleanest URL-state handling I have measured in this app
   and the drill-down is built on it for free.
2. **The row descriptions.** Every one of the 34 rows says what *changing it does*, not what it is —
   "Changing it breaks existing links", "Noisy on a daily schedule", "The one alert most teams keep
   on". A settings hub whose consequences you have to guess is a trap, and this one is not.
3. **The two destructive `consequence` sentences** (`settingsCatalog.ts:200-201`, `:334-335`). They
   name what is destroyed, what is explicitly *not* destroyed, and that there is no undo and no grace
   period — and the `desc`/`consequence` agreement rule that stops the dialog contradicting the row
   is written into the type (`:40-45`). This is the best destructive copy in the product.
4. **The whole D06 guard**: quieter destructive button → `ConfirmModal` → soft error alert, inside a
   card wearing the **same** `.reg-danger` error border as `SourceDetailView.astro:271`. Identical
   treatment for the destructive part of a page wherever it appears. Do not re-style it.
5. **Identity is a prop.** `SettingsSubject` (`settingsCatalog.ts:95-100`) — the page that is *about*
   the signed-in account reads it from the same `Astro.locals` the sidebar does. J08-F3 is closed and
   must not regress.
6. **The 44px tap-target floor.** Measured at 390: every toggle `<label>`, every row button and the
   back link are 44×44 or 44 high, achieved by growing the box around a `toggle-sm` rather than
   growing the switch (`SettingsView.astro:182-190`). Keep the mechanism; F5/F6 do not touch it.
7. **`.set-flag` uses `opacity`, not `display`.** The reason is written down (`:371-374`): a hub that
   twitches on every toggle reads as unstable. F5 must preserve that property — take the box out of
   flow, do not switch to `display`.
8. **The narrow-tier CSS is documented at the level of *why the rule can win*** — specificity
   arithmetic against the shared `pattern-mobile-row` rule, the media query written as the exact
   negation of the one product boundary in `lib/mobileNav.ts`, and the block placed last on purpose
   (`:292-299`, `:322-325`, `:403-405`). Two of my findings are dead rules in this same file, and the
   fact that I could diagnose both from the comments is the reason.
9. **The deferred-action honesty convention.** A button whose destination does not exist prints a
   sentence saying where it goes and why it does not go there (`settingsControls.ts:189-197`). It is
   worth more to a reviewer than a disabled control, and F12 is a request to *extend* it, not replace
   it.
10. **The absorption of `/profile`.** No dead link, no redirect stub, the sidebar entry retargeted in
    both its expanded and rail forms, and the reason recorded in three places. The route is a clean
    404 because nothing points at it. This is how a page should be removed.
