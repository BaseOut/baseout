1 · What Baseout Is

Baseout is the backup, restore, and data intelligence layer for Airtable. It automatically backs up everything in a customer's Airtable estate — schema, records, attachments, and (via guided submission) automations and interfaces — to storage the customer controls or to Baseout-managed storage. It restores any of it, point-in-time, without ever overwriting live data. And on top of the backup pipeline it builds what Airtable itself doesn't offer: an auto-generated visual map of your schema, a change history of everything, a health score for every base, documentation tooling, and a live SQL copy of your data you can query directly.

Elevator pitch on file (a starting point, not a mandate): "Baseout is the backup, restore, and data intelligence layer for Airtable — giving platform admins real-time protection, schema visibility, and direct SQL access to their own data."

Business context — an established player relaunching, not a cold start

Baseout is the full rebrand and next-generation replacement of On2Air Backups, a known name in the Airtable ecosystem with ~200 paying customers who will migrate to Baseout. For GTM this means: (1) existing brand equity and community trust to transfer — and a legacy base whose migration must feel like an upgrade, not a rug-pull; (2) real usage history — we know what actual Airtable admins back up, how often, and how big their estates are (data available for ICP work); (3) a proven but thin category — customers already pay for Airtable backup, the closest competitor went out of business, and Airtable's own snapshots are the "do nothing" default.

Where it's going (V2 trajectory — vision material, not launch claims)

Multi-platform Spaces (Airtable + Notion + HubSpot under one roof) · an MCP server and RAG pipeline exposing the customer's data layer to AI assistants · a governance/compliance suite · third-party connectors (Zapier, Make). Positioning should have room for this arc without leaning on it at launch.

Structural note: the first two jobs are served by static backup (files to storage); the last two require dynamic backup (a hosted database copy). Claims inventory — verify every "only" before print

  • Only backup solution focused exclusively on Airtable — depth story: field types, rate limits, attachment-expiry quirks handled natively.
  • Most storage destinations of any vendor; the only one supporting OneDrive.
  • Only vendor offering both bring-your-own-storage AND bring-your-own-database — competitors are consolidating onto their own hosted storage.
  • On static/BYOS plans, customer record data is never stored on Baseout servers. Must always be scoped to static/BYOS mode — as a blanket claim it would be false for dynamic customers.
  • Only solution turning Airtable into a live SQL layer (post-Sync Inc); scope carefully against sync products.
  • Restore never overwrites live data — safety claim, uncontested.

The static-vs-dynamic tension: the privacy claim (static) and hosted-SQL claim (dynamic) are both flagship differentiators describing opposite data postures. Working frame: customer choice — "your data, your rules: keep it fully external, or let us host a copy and make it queryable." Trust assets: ~200 paying customers and years of operating history, encryption everywhere, provable per-run audit reports. Liabilities to manage: the rebrand itself (continuity messaging so the community doesn't read "shutdown"), SOC 2 not yet certified, several headline features (restore!) still in build — §6 claim hygiene.

5 · Audience Raw Material (for the ICP Decision)

The persona on file: the Airtable Platform Admin

The person responsible for building, managing, and protecting an organization's Airtable infrastructure: internal IT managers, RevOps/BizOps owners, and independent Airtable consultants managing multiple client accounts. Manages one or more workspaces on behalf of an organization or clients; needs reliable, auditable, low-maintenance backup; cares about data integrity, schema visibility, fast recovery. Technically capable — comfortable with OAuth, storage configuration, SQL. Extends naturally to Notion/HubSpot/Salesforce admins as platforms are added (V2). Secondary personas (V2): developers building on the SQL layer; enterprise IT/compliance officers.

6 · Messaging Ingredients & Practical Tips

Funnel mechanics already in the product

Pre-signup schema visualization is the designed hook: OAuth in, see your schema diagram, then hit "Start backing up your data" — value in 60 seconds, zero commitment. Passwordless magic-link signup, no credit card — friction claims are legitimate. The trial runs a real backup on the customer's own data — "see your actual data protected" beats a demo (trial shape may change with pricing work; keep copy flexible). The product will also run embedded inside Airtable as an extension (in build) — meet users where they already are.

Tone & design direction (decided product-side; messaging should match)

The product is deliberately a professional operations console: functional over decorative, information-dense, trust signals first (backup status, last run, success/failure always visible), power over simplicity. The audience is technical and accountable. Messaging that reads like a consumer lifestyle product will clash with the product experience. Think: calm, competent, specific; numbers and receipts over adjectives.

The legacy-migration narrative (a launch-critical audience of ~200)

On2Air customers move to Baseout via a guided re-auth flow. Internal draft messaging emphasizes: static backups work exactly the same — same destinations, same process; no more artificial limits; new capabilities await when you're ready. Pricing commitments for this cohort belong to the pricing engagement — coordinate the two workstreams so the migration email says one coherent thing. The rebrand announcement is also a community moment (ecosystem podcasts, forums) — "On2Air grew up" is a story, not just a notice.

Claim hygiene (legal/accuracy guardrails) — (1) Never claim SOC 2 certified until it is; "SOC 2 in progress" is the approved formulation. (2) Scope the privacy claim ("never stores your data") to static/BYOS mode explicitly. (3) Restore, retention, instant backup, the SQL layer, and most intelligence features are still in build — launch copy must track the §3 status flags or clearly mark "coming soon." (4) V2 items (MCP/RAG, governance, connectors, multi-platform) are vision-tier only — roadmap slides yes, feature pages no. (5) Verify every "only vendor that…" claim against the current market before print.

Analytics & attribution already planned: PostHog (product analytics, funnels, session replay) and dub.co (referral/affiliate tracking, migrating from Rewardful — existing affiliate links must keep working) — campaign measurement and a referral program have plumbing on the roadmap.
